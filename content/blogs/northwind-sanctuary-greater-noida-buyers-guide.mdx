Most project brochures are written to be admired, not read. This one is worth reading — because NorthWind Sanctuary publishes the things buyers usually have to extract by phone: the exact unit break-up of each tower, the carpet and built-up area of every typical apartment, the full 47-element master plan legend, and a specification table split tower by tower.

This guide walks through what that document tells you, what it deliberately leaves out, and how to use both when you are deciding between Amaltas, Banyan and Cedar.

## What NorthWind Sanctuary Actually Is

<a id="about"></a>

NorthWind Sanctuary is a low-density residential development at **Plot 07, Sector Pi-1, Greater Noida, Uttar Pradesh 201306**, by **Northwind Estates Pvt. Ltd.** It sits on a 4.48-acre parcel and carries just three towers — Tower A (Amaltas), Tower B (Banyan) and Tower C (Cedar) — for a total of 185 homes.

Read those figures together and the design intent becomes obvious. One hundred and eighty-five families across 4.48 acres, with 80% of the ground given back to landscape, is a deliberately thin spread — the arithmetic of a project that would rather build up than build over. The tallest towers reach 20 floors; Amaltas stops at 17.

The architecture is by **Confluence**, a multidisciplinary practice founded in 1999 by Vineeta and Vishal, now more than 25 years old with over 200 architects and designers on the roll. The tagline the developer has chosen — *Live Rare* — is doing real work here: scarcity of neighbours is the product.

## The Three Towers, Compared

<a id="towers"></a>

This is the decision most buyers are actually making. The three towers are not three price points of the same apartment — they are three different homes, and the differences are structural.

### Tower A — Amaltas · 4 BHK + Servant

Seventeen floors, 31 units in total: 28 luxury apartments, 1 penthouse and 2 garden villas. The typical unit is the largest at Sanctuary — **3,750 sq. ft. saleable, 1,956 sq. ft. carpet, 2,839 sq. ft. built-up, with 705 sq. ft. of balcony**.

Three things separate Amaltas from the rest. It is the only tower with a servant room in the typical plan. It carries **705 sq. ft. of balcony** — more outdoor area than many two-bedroom apartments have indoors. And it is the only tower specified with **VRV air-conditioning** throughout; Banyan and Cedar use split units. With 31 homes across 17 floors, it is also the least crowded lift lobby on site.

### Tower B — Banyan · 4 BHK

Twenty floors, 76 units: 72 luxury apartments, 2 penthouses and 2 garden villas. The typical unit is **2,431 sq. ft. saleable, 1,286 sq. ft. carpet, 1,800 sq. ft. built-up, with 394 sq. ft. of balcony**.

Banyan is the four-bedroom home for buyers who want the bedroom count without the Amaltas footprint. It is planned around a 21'-0" x 15'-9" drawing and dining hall, with a master bedroom and dresser, a dedicated puja area, four balconies off the living spaces and a separate utility balcony.

### Tower C — Cedar · 3 BHK

Twenty floors, 78 units: 76 luxury apartments and 2 penthouses. No garden villas. The typical unit is **1,895 sq. ft. saleable, 957 sq. ft. carpet, 1,468 sq. ft. built-up, with 382 sq. ft. of balcony**.

Cedar is the most efficient footprint on site: a 19'-0" x 10'-11" combined living and dining space, a master bedroom with dresser and attached toilet, three balconies plus a utility balcony. It is the natural choice for a family of three or four who want the address and the amenity deck without paying for a fourth bedroom they will use as a store room.

## Saleable, Built-Up, Carpet: How to Read the Numbers

<a id="areas"></a>

Almost every dispute a buyer has after booking begins here. The brochure prints all four figures for every unit, so use them.

<Callout type="note">
  **Super area** is the whole flat measured to the outside of its walls — including the area under columns, cupboards, window projections and balconies — *plus* your proportionate share of lobbies, staircases, lifts, shafts, corridors, stilts, the lift machine room, water supply arrangements, the maintenance office and the security and fire control rooms. It is the number the price is usually quoted against, and it is the number that includes things you will never furnish.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  **Built-up area** follows the CREDAI definition: the total polyline area measured on the outer line of the unit, including balconies and/or terrace, with or without roof. Outer walls shared with another unit count at 50%; the remaining outer walls count at 100%.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  **Carpet area** is the net usable floor area — external walls, service shafts, exclusive balconies and exclusive open terraces are all excluded, though internal partition walls are included. This is the floor you will actually walk on.
</Callout>

Now apply it. In Amaltas, 3,750 sq. ft. saleable resolves to 1,956 sq. ft. of carpet. In Banyan, 2,431 becomes 1,286. In Cedar, 1,895 becomes 957. Across all three towers, carpet lands at roughly half the saleable figure — which is unremarkable for a high-rise with this much common amenity, but it is exactly why you should compare **carpet against carpet** when you benchmark Sanctuary against anything else in Greater Noida. Comparing saleable to saleable across two projects with different loading is comparing nothing at all.

The balcony figures deserve the same attention, because balconies are excluded from carpet and included in built-up. Amaltas gives you 705 sq. ft. of it. If open space is what you are buying, that number matters more than the headline.

## The Seven Zones

<a id="zones"></a>

The master plan legend runs to 47 numbered elements, organised into seven zones. This is the part of the brochure most worth reading slowly, because it tells you what the 80% landscape is actually made of.

**Zone 1 — The Arrival Zone.** Entrance plaza, club drop-off, commercial plaza, separate drop-offs for Towers A, B and C, surface parking, the ramp to the basement, a palmetum, and a feature wall flaunted with a water feature. *"At Sanctuary, your arrival is more than just an entrance, it's your first step into a world of elegance."*

**Zone 2 — The Recreational Zone.** Banquet lawn, café spill-over space, central lawn, callistemon garden, bamboo walk, reflexology pathway, and a sculpture court with a reading area.

**Zone 3 — The Active Zone.** Exercise station, hedge garden, yoga and dance deck.

**Zone 4 — Fun & Entertainment Zone.** Main pool, party pool, kids' pool, shallow pool, parasols, a waterfall from the sky bridge, and a party deck with F&B.

**Zone 5 — Sports Zone.** The densest zone on the plan: skating rink, badminton court, pickle ball court, cricket pitch, multi-purpose play court, putting golf, jogging track, outdoor gym, kids' play arena, and a tree court with a pet park.

**Zone 6 — The Serenity Walk.** The serenity walkway itself, a tree house, a waterfall, a tree orchard, a pet park and a sky gazing deck.

**Zone 7 — Under the Serenity Walk.** The shaded level below: hopscotch, a musical garden, an outdoor gym, table tennis, swings, and a chaupal with seating.

<Callout type="info">
  Note the vertical logic: Zone 6 and Zone 7 occupy the same footprint at different levels. The Serenity Walk is elevated, and the play and gathering spaces tuck in beneath it. On a 4.48-acre site, that is how you fit 47 elements in without paving the garden.
</Callout>

## The Green Case: IGBC Pre-Certified Platinum

<a id="green"></a>

NorthWind Sanctuary is described as **one of the first IGBC Pre-Certified Platinum Rated group housing societies in Greater Noida**. Two words in that phrase are load-bearing, and a serious buyer should hold on to both.

*Pre-certified* means the rating has been awarded on the design, not yet on the finished building. That is normal and it is a genuine signal — Platinum is the top IGBC band, and you do not get design pre-certification by accident. But the final rating is confirmed after construction. Ask the developer, in writing, what the pre-certification covers and what the plan is for converting it into a final rating.

The 80% lush green landscape figure is the other half of the argument, and it is the half you can verify with your own eyes at the site.

## What the Brochure Does Not Tell You — And What to Ask

<a id="ask"></a>

Here is the honest part. The NorthWind Sanctuary brochure is unusually detailed on design and unusually silent on commerce. It contains **no price, no per-square-foot rate, no payment plan, no RERA registration number and no possession date**. That is not an oversight you should read as evasion — those figures move, and a printed brochure is the wrong place for them — but it does mean four things belong on your list before you sign anything.
