  NorthWind Sanctuary, the residential development by Northwind Estates Pvt. Ltd. at Plot 07, Sector Pi-1, Greater Noida, stands as one of the first IGBC Pre-Certified Platinum Rated group housing societies in Greater Noida. For a market where "green" is too often a brochure adjective, that is a distinction with a paper trail behind it — a rating awarded against a published scorecard, not a marketing claim.

  It is also the clearest possible statement of what the project is trying to be. Sanctuary is not a density play dressed up with a few planters. The Platinum pre-certification sits on top of a masterplan in which four-fifths of the land is landscape, three towers hold just 185 homes across 4.48 acres, and the open ground is organised into seven distinct zones rather than one leftover lawn.

  ## What an IGBC Platinum pre-certification actually means
  <a id="igbc-platinum"></a>

  The Indian Green Building Council — the Confederation of Indian Industry body that administers green building ratings in India — rates residential projects across four tiers, of which Platinum is the highest. A **pre-certification** is issued at the design stage: the project's drawings, systems and specifications are assessed against the rating criteria and awarded a rating before the first slab is cast, with final certification following on completed construction.

  The practical significance is timing. A green rating pursued after a building is finished can only certify what was already built. A pre-certification means the environmental performance targets were written into the design brief — into the orientation of the towers, the extent of soft landscape, the water and energy systems, the choice of materials and the quality of daylight and ventilation inside each home — from the beginning. For a buyer, it is the difference between a building that was made sustainable and one that was designed that way.

  ## Eighty per cent green — the number that shapes everything
  <a id="green-landscape"></a>

  The headline figure on the Sanctuary plan is 80% lush green landscape. It is worth sitting with what that implies on a 4.48-acre parcel in one of the fastest-densifying corridors in the National Capital Region: the built footprint is deliberately small, parking is pushed into the basement, and the ground plane is returned almost entirely to planting, water and walking.

  That decision cascades through everything else. A predominantly soft, planted site absorbs rainwater instead of shedding it. A tree canopy and a palmetum moderate surface temperatures around the towers. Sight lines from the apartments run across landscape rather than into a neighbour's balcony. The green cover is not an amenity bolted on at the end — it is the organising logic of the plan, and it is a substantial part of why the Platinum rating is defensible.

  ## Seven zones, one continuous landscape
  <a id="seven-zones"></a>

  Rather than a single central green, the Sanctuary masterplan divides its open ground into seven landscaped zones, each with its own character and its own set of elements — forty-seven of them in all, itemised on the master plan legend.

  - **Zone 1 — The Arrival Zone:** entrance plaza, club drop-off, commercial plaza, tower drop-offs, palmetum and a feature wall flaunted with a water feature.
  - **Zone 2 — The Recreational Zone:** banquet lawn, central lawn, café spill-over space, Callistemon garden, bamboo walk, reflexology pathway and a sculpture court with reading area.
  - **Zone 3 — The Active Zone:** exercise station, hedge garden and a yoga / dance deck.
  - **Zone 4 — The Fun & Entertainment Zone:** main pool, party pool, kids' pool, shallow pool, parasols, a waterfall from the sky bridge and a party deck.
  - **Zone 5 — The Sports Zone:** skating rink, badminton court, pickle ball court, cricket pitch, multi-purpose play court, jogging track, outdoor gym, kids' play arena and a tree court with pet park.
  - **Zone 6 — The Serenity Walk:** the walkway itself, a tree house, waterfall, tree orchard, pet park and a sky gazing deck.
  - **Zone 7 — Under the Serenity Walk:** hopscotch, musical garden, outdoor gym, table tennis, swings and a chaupal with seating.

  The zoning does real work. It lets a cricket pitch and a reading garden coexist on a compact site without either one ruining the other, and it means the landscape is used across the whole day and the whole age range — which is, in the end, what keeps a green space genuinely green rather than ornamental.

  ## Low density by design
  <a id="low-density"></a>

  The arithmetic of Sanctuary is unusually restrained. Three towers — Amaltas, Banyan and Cedar — hold 180 luxury apartments and garden villas plus 05 penthouses: 185 homes in total, on 4.48 acres, rising to a maximum of 20 floors.

  Low density is not a luxury flourish here; it is a precondition. Fewer homes on a larger green means fewer cars on the surface, less hard paving, less load on the site's water and waste systems, and more room for the tree canopy that a Platinum-rated landscape depends on. The IGBC rating and the low-density plan are two readings of the same decision.

  ## The Confluence masterplan
  <a id="confluence"></a>

  The design is by **Confluence**, the award-winning multidisciplinary practice founded in 1999 by Vineeta and Vishal. With more than 25 years of work behind it and a studio of over 200 architects and designers, the firm brought architecture, interiors and landscape into a single brief at Sanctuary — which is precisely how an 80% green site and a Platinum-rated envelope end up reinforcing each other instead of competing for the same ground.

  Northwind Estates describes its own position in similar terms: a brand "rooted in over two decades of real estate legacy", with "an unwavering focus on design integrity, sustainability, and transparency". The IGBC Platinum pre-certification is the first external test of that claim, and Sanctuary has passed it at the design stage — before a single buyer had to take the promise on trust.
